# Build 0.0.5 — Integrity

## Status

**Active Discovery**

## Purpose

This build defines how ClawForge governs meaningful changes to itself.

Build 0.0.4 established that Orientation must precede responsible participation. Orientation provides sufficient understanding of ClawForge's current state, governing foundation, prior decisions, and present objective.

Understanding alone, however, does not determine whether a proposed change should become part of ClawForge.

Build 0.0.5 therefore explores the integrity gate that must exist between a proposed change and its acceptance into ClawForge.

## Scope

This build applies to meaningful proposed changes to ClawForge itself, including changes to:

- the Foundation
- First Principles
- governance
- Architecture Decision Records
- authoritative architecture
- implementation
- repository documentation
- build and historical records

This build does not attempt to evaluate every question asked of a future ClawForge agent.

It also does not yet define how ClawForge certifies an external system or agent.

Those capabilities may derive from this work, but they require separate architectural treatment.

## Core Question

> What must ClawForge verify before allowing a meaningful proposed change to become part of ClawForge?

## Emerging Principle

Permission to proceed should be proportional to the quality of orientation and the consequence of the proposed change.

A meaningful change must not be accepted merely because a participant proposes it or because the change is technically possible.

ClawForge must determine whether the proposal is sufficiently understood, appropriately governed, internally consistent, traceable, and responsible before allowing forward progress.

## Meaningful Change

A proposed change is meaningful when it could alter ClawForge's identity, governing interpretation, authority, obligations, architecture, behavior, historical record, or the basis for future decisions.

The Integrity gate applies whether a proposed change:

- contradicts ClawForge
- extends ClawForge
- clarifies ClawForge
- implements ClawForge
- changes how ClawForge is interpreted or applied

Meaningful changes include changes affecting:

- the Foundation
- First Principles
- governance
- Orientation
- Architecture Decision Records
- authority, permission, approval, or override
- required workflows and verification
- implementation behavior
- durable historical records
- how future participants understand ClawForge

A correction may receive lightweight review when it can be demonstrated that it does not alter meaning, behavior, authority, governance, or historical interpretation.

The participant proposing a change must not have sole authority to classify that change as non-meaningful.

ClawForge must verify the proposed impact classification.

When the impact of a change is uncertain, ClawForge must treat the change as meaningful until sufficient understanding establishes otherwise.

## Integrity Checks

Before a meaningful proposed change may receive `PROCEED`, ClawForge must evaluate eight integrity checks.

### 1. Orientation

ClawForge must establish that the proposal is being evaluated against a sufficiently current and complete understanding of the project.

### 2. Intent

The proposed change, its purpose, and the problem it is intended to address must be clearly defined.

### 3. Governance

The applicable Foundation, First Principles, governance requirements, Architecture Decision Records, and other authoritative commitments must be identified and followed.

### 4. Consistency

The proposal must be evaluated for contradictions, silent changes, unresolved conflicts, and unintended effects on existing ClawForge commitments.

### 5. Process

The proposal must use the correct artifact, workflow, review path, and implementation method for the type and consequence of the change.

### 6. Traceability

Future participants must be able to understand what changed, why it changed, what governed the decision, and how the result was reached.

### 7. Consequence

The reasonably foreseeable effects, risks, dependencies, reversibility, and long-term implications of the proposal must be sufficiently understood.

### 8. Authority

The participants proposing, approving, implementing, and overriding the change must act within clearly established authority.

## Integrity Decision Rule

The integrity checks must be interpreted through ClawForge's current purpose, Foundation, First Principles, governance, Architecture Decision Records, authoritative architecture, and durable history.

Evidence must support each determination.

Every applicable integrity check must be satisfied before a meaningful proposed change receives `PROCEED`.

A check may be identified as not applicable only when the reason is explicitly stated and its exclusion does not create an integrity gap.

When evidence is insufficient, governing sources conflict, applicability is uncertain, or any required check remains unsatisfied, the result must be `HOLD`.

A `PROCEED` result does not declare that a proposal is perfect or permanently correct.

It establishes that the proposal is sufficiently oriented, understood, governed, consistent, traceable, responsible, and authorized to continue through the applicable ClawForge process.

## HOLD Result

A `HOLD` is not a vague rejection.

It is an integrity decision stating that a meaningful proposed change is not yet sufficiently ready, governed, supported, consistent, traceable, responsible, or authorized to proceed.

Every `HOLD` must communicate:

- the integrity check or checks that were not satisfied
- the specific reason the proposal cannot currently proceed
- the governing basis for the decision
- the evidence supporting the decision or the evidence that is missing
- the action required to address the hold
- the conditions under which the proposal may be evaluated again

A `HOLD` must be specific, evidence-based, governed, and actionable.

Whenever responsible progress remains possible, the result must explain a reasonable path toward re-evaluation.

Some proposed changes may be fundamentally incompatible with ClawForge's purpose, Foundation, First Principles, governance, or established architectural commitments.

In those cases, the `HOLD` must identify the incompatibility clearly rather than presenting routine corrections as though they would make the proposal acceptable.

A `HOLD` protects ClawForge's integrity while preserving responsible forward progress wherever that progress remains possible.

## PROCEED Result

A `PROCEED` establishes that a meaningful proposed change is sufficiently ready to continue through a specific next step in the applicable ClawForge process.

It is scoped approval, not unlimited or permanent authorization.

Every `PROCEED` must identify:

- the specific proposal and version evaluated
- the next action being authorized
- the governing ClawForge state used during evaluation
- the evidence and assumptions supporting the decision
- the authority under which the action may proceed
- any limits, dependencies, or required follow-up
- the conditions that would make the decision stale or invalid

A `PROCEED` does not declare that a proposal is perfect, permanently correct, or approved for every future use.

It does not authorize unrelated changes, later versions, additional artifacts, broader authority, or actions beyond the evaluated scope.

A material change to the proposal, evidence, governing context, authority, dependencies, or reasonably foreseeable consequences requires re-evaluation.

Any condition that must be satisfied before the authorized action may begin must produce a `HOLD` until that condition is satisfied.

A `PROCEED` may require follow-up after the authorized step, but required follow-up must not be used to bypass an unmet prerequisite.

A `PROCEED` authorizes the evaluated next step.

It does not by itself establish that the resulting implementation faithfully matches the approved proposal or may be permanently accepted into ClawForge.

## Verification and Finalization

A `PROCEED` authorizes implementation of the evaluated next step.

It does not automatically authorize acceptance of the implemented result into ClawForge's authoritative state.

After implementation, the result must be verified and deliberately finalized.

### Verification

Verification determines whether the actual result faithfully satisfies the proposal, scope, conditions, and authority that received `PROCEED`.

Verification must evaluate whether:

- the implemented result matches the approved proposal and version
- the implementation remained within the authorized scope
- all prerequisites, limits, dependencies, and required conditions were satisfied
- intended behavior and actual behavior are sufficiently aligned
- no material change was introduced without evaluation
- required documentation, evidence, tests, and historical records are complete
- the applicable integrity checks remain satisfied
- the governing ClawForge context has not materially changed since approval

A material difference between the approved proposal and the implemented result invalidates the prior `PROCEED` for that difference.

When verification cannot establish that the result faithfully satisfies the approval, the result must be `HOLD`.

The `HOLD` must explain whether the implementation requires correction, additional evidence, renewed evaluation, or a new proposal.

### Finalization

Finalization deliberately incorporates a verified result into ClawForge's authoritative state and durable history.

A meaningful change must not be finalized until verification is complete.

Finalization must:

- identify the result and version being accepted
- record the basis and evidence for acceptance
- update all affected authoritative artifacts
- preserve what changed and why
- update applicable build, architectural, governance, and historical records
- close, fulfill, or supersede the approval used for the change
- identify any remaining obligations or required follow-up
- establish the new ClawForge state against which future work will orient

Finalization does not erase the proposal, approval, implementation history, disagreement, uncertainty, or prior state that led to the result.

No meaningful change becomes part of ClawForge's authoritative state merely because it was proposed, approved, implemented, or committed.

It must be verified and deliberately finalized.

## Architectural Boundary

Build 0.0.5 governs changes to ClawForge.

A future build may define how ClawForge verifies that another system or agent faithfully implements ClawForge.

A verified agent may then reason about user questions within its established scope, authority, and operating contract.

These are related layers, but they are not the same checker.
