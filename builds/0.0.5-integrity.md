# Build 0.0.5 — Integrity

## Status

**Complete**

## Purpose

This build defines how ClawForge governs meaningful changes to itself.

Build 0.0.4 established that Orientation must precede responsible participation. Orientation provides sufficient understanding of ClawForge's current state, governing foundation, prior decisions, and present objective.

Understanding alone, however, does not determine whether a proposed change should become part of ClawForge.

Build 0.0.5 therefore explores the integrity gate that must exist between a proposed change and its acceptance into ClawForge.

## Scope

This build applies to meaningful proposed changes to ClawForge itself, including changes to:

- the Foundation
- First Principles
- governance
- Architecture Decision Records
- authoritative architecture
- implementation
- repository documentation
- build and historical records

This build does not attempt to evaluate every question asked of a future ClawForge agent.

It also does not yet define how ClawForge certifies an external system or agent.

Those capabilities may derive from this work, but they require separate architectural treatment.

## Core Question

> What must ClawForge verify before allowing a meaningful proposed change to become part of ClawForge?

## Emerging Principle

Permission to proceed should be proportional to the quality of orientation and the consequence of the proposed change.

A meaningful change must not be accepted merely because a participant proposes it or because the change is technically possible.

ClawForge must determine whether the proposal is sufficiently understood, appropriately governed, internally consistent, traceable, and responsible before allowing forward progress.

## Meaningful Change

A proposed change is meaningful when it could alter ClawForge's identity, governing interpretation, authority, obligations, architecture, behavior, historical record, or the basis for future decisions.

The Integrity gate applies whether a proposed change:

- contradicts ClawForge
- extends ClawForge
- clarifies ClawForge
- implements ClawForge
- changes how ClawForge is interpreted or applied

Meaningful changes include changes affecting:

- the Foundation
- First Principles
- governance
- Orientation
- Architecture Decision Records
- authority, permission, approval, or override
- required workflows and verification
- implementation behavior
- durable historical records
- how future participants understand ClawForge

A correction may receive lightweight review when it can be demonstrated that it does not alter meaning, behavior, authority, governance, or historical interpretation.

The participant proposing a change must not have sole authority to classify that change as non-meaningful.

ClawForge must verify the proposed impact classification.

When the impact of a change is uncertain, ClawForge must treat the change as meaningful until sufficient understanding establishes otherwise.

## Integrity Checks

Before a meaningful proposed change may receive `PROCEED`, ClawForge must evaluate eight integrity checks.

### 1. Orientation

ClawForge must establish that the proposal is being evaluated against a sufficiently current and complete understanding of the project.

### 2. Intent

The proposed change, its purpose, and the problem it is intended to address must be clearly defined.

### 3. Governance

The applicable Foundation, First Principles, governance requirements, Architecture Decision Records, and other authoritative commitments must be identified and followed.

### 4. Consistency

The proposal must be evaluated for contradictions, silent changes, unresolved conflicts, and unintended effects on existing ClawForge commitments.

### 5. Process

The proposal must use the correct artifact, workflow, review path, and implementation method for the type and consequence of the change.

### 6. Traceability

Future participants must be able to understand what changed, why it changed, what governed the decision, and how the result was reached.

### 7. Consequence

The reasonably foreseeable effects, risks, dependencies, reversibility, and long-term implications of the proposal must be sufficiently understood.

### 8. Authority

The participants proposing, approving, implementing, and overriding the change must act within clearly established authority.

## Integrity Decision Rule

The integrity checks must be interpreted through ClawForge's current purpose, Foundation, First Principles, governance, Architecture Decision Records, authoritative architecture, and durable history.

Evidence must support each determination.

Every applicable integrity check must be satisfied before a meaningful proposed change receives `PROCEED`.

A check may be identified as not applicable only when the reason is explicitly stated and its exclusion does not create an integrity gap.

When evidence is insufficient, governing sources conflict, applicability is uncertain, or any required check remains unsatisfied, the result must be `HOLD`.

A `PROCEED` result does not declare that a proposal is perfect or permanently correct.

It establishes that the proposal is sufficiently oriented, understood, governed, consistent, traceable, responsible, and authorized to continue through the applicable ClawForge process.

## HOLD Result

A `HOLD` is not a vague rejection.

It is an integrity decision stating that a meaningful proposed change is not yet sufficiently ready, governed, supported, consistent, traceable, responsible, or authorized to proceed.

Every `HOLD` must communicate:

- the integrity check or checks that were not satisfied
- the specific reason the proposal cannot currently proceed
- the governing basis for the decision
- the evidence supporting the decision or the evidence that is missing
- the action required to address the hold
- the conditions under which the proposal may be evaluated again

A `HOLD` must be specific, evidence-based, governed, and actionable.

Whenever responsible progress remains possible, the result must explain a reasonable path toward re-evaluation.

Some proposed changes may be fundamentally incompatible with ClawForge's purpose, Foundation, First Principles, governance, or established architectural commitments.

In those cases, the `HOLD` must identify the incompatibility clearly rather than presenting routine corrections as though they would make the proposal acceptable.

A `HOLD` protects ClawForge's integrity while preserving responsible forward progress wherever that progress remains possible.

## PROCEED Result

A `PROCEED` establishes that a meaningful proposed change is sufficiently ready to continue through a specific next step in the applicable ClawForge process.

It is scoped approval, not unlimited or permanent authorization.

Every `PROCEED` must identify:

- the specific proposal and version evaluated
- the next action being authorized
- the governing ClawForge state used during evaluation
- the evidence and assumptions supporting the decision
- the authority under which the action may proceed
- any limits, dependencies, or required follow-up
- the conditions that would make the decision stale or invalid

A `PROCEED` does not declare that a proposal is perfect, permanently correct, or approved for every future use.

It does not authorize unrelated changes, later versions, additional artifacts, broader authority, or actions beyond the evaluated scope.

A material change to the proposal, evidence, governing context, authority, dependencies, or reasonably foreseeable consequences requires re-evaluation.

Any condition that must be satisfied before the authorized action may begin must produce a `HOLD` until that condition is satisfied.

A `PROCEED` may require follow-up after the authorized step, but required follow-up must not be used to bypass an unmet prerequisite.

A `PROCEED` authorizes the evaluated next step.

It does not by itself establish that the resulting implementation faithfully matches the approved proposal or may be permanently accepted into ClawForge.

## Verification and Finalization

A `PROCEED` authorizes implementation of the evaluated next step.

It does not automatically authorize acceptance of the implemented result into ClawForge's authoritative state.

After implementation, the result must be verified and deliberately finalized.

### Verification

Verification determines whether the actual result faithfully satisfies the proposal, scope, conditions, and authority that received `PROCEED`.

Verification must evaluate whether:

- the implemented result matches the approved proposal and version
- the implementation remained within the authorized scope
- all prerequisites, limits, dependencies, and required conditions were satisfied
- intended behavior and actual behavior are sufficiently aligned
- no material change was introduced without evaluation
- required documentation, evidence, tests, and historical records are complete
- the applicable integrity checks remain satisfied
- the governing ClawForge context has not materially changed since approval

A material difference between the approved proposal and the implemented result invalidates the prior `PROCEED` for that difference.

When verification cannot establish that the result faithfully satisfies the approval, the result must be `HOLD`.

The `HOLD` must explain whether the implementation requires correction, additional evidence, renewed evaluation, or a new proposal.

### Finalization

Finalization deliberately incorporates a verified result into ClawForge's authoritative state and durable history.

A meaningful change must not be finalized until verification is complete.

Finalization must:

- identify the result and version being accepted
- record the basis and evidence for acceptance
- update all affected authoritative artifacts
- preserve what changed and why
- update applicable build, architectural, governance, and historical records
- close, fulfill, or supersede the approval used for the change
- identify any remaining obligations or required follow-up
- establish the new ClawForge state against which future work will orient

Finalization does not erase the proposal, approval, implementation history, disagreement, uncertainty, or prior state that led to the result.

No meaningful change becomes part of ClawForge's authoritative state merely because it was proposed, approved, implemented, or committed.

It must be verified and deliberately finalized.

## Integrity Memory

Every meaningful change must produce a durable and inspectable Integrity Record.

An Integrity Record is more than an activity log.

It preserves ClawForge's memory of:

- what was proposed
- what ClawForge understood at the time
- which authoritative state governed the evaluation
- which principles, decisions, architecture, and evidence applied
- what uncertainty, disagreement, assumptions, or missing information existed
- why the proposal received `HOLD` or `PROCEED`
- what action was authorized
- what was actually implemented
- how the result was verified
- why and under whose authority it was finalized
- what new authoritative state resulted

Each meaningful change should have one identifiable Integrity Record that follows the proposal through its complete lifecycle.

The record must identify:

- the proposal and exact version evaluated
- its impact classification
- the authoritative pre-change state
- the applicable governing artifacts
- findings for each applicable integrity check
- evidence supporting each determination
- unresolved uncertainty and disagreement
- the participants, roles, and authority involved
- the decision and its scope
- Challenge Review findings when required
- the implemented result
- verification findings
- finalization authority
- the resulting authoritative state
- remaining obligations or required follow-up

Earlier reasoning must not be silently rewritten after it has supported an Integrity decision.

New evidence, corrections, changed conclusions, implementation differences, or later decisions must be appended, explicitly supersede an earlier entry, or trigger renewed evaluation.

The record must preserve what ClawForge understood and decided at each stage, including conclusions later shown to be incomplete or incorrect.

Lightweight Review also requires a durable record sufficient to identify:

- the exact correction
- the affected artifact
- why it was classified as non-meaningful
- who or what performed the classification
- confirmation that implementation remained within the approved lightweight scope

Build 0.0.5 defines the required memory contract.

The storage format may later use Markdown, structured data, commit metadata, or a combination of forms, provided the memory remains durable, inspectable, traceable, and usable by future humans and intelligences.

Finalization adds the completed Integrity Record to ClawForge's durable memory.

That memory becomes part of the authoritative context used by future Orientation and Integrity evaluation.

## ClawForge as Evaluator

ClawForge performs the Integrity evaluation.

A human, AI, agent, implementation, or other participant may initiate and execute the evaluation, but the result must be produced through ClawForge's explicit governing process.

No participant may substitute personal assertion, status, technical capability, or ownership for an Integrity decision.

The evaluator must apply:

- ClawForge's authoritative pre-change state
- the applicable governing artifacts
- the eight integrity checks
- the available evidence
- the authority established by governance
- the scope and consequence of the proposed change

The proposal must not be evaluated according to rules introduced by the proposal itself.

A change becomes authoritative only after it has received `PROCEED`, been implemented within scope, passed verification, and been deliberately finalized.

The evaluation sequence is therefore:

```text
AUTHORITATIVE PRE-CHANGE STATE
            |
            v
EVALUATE PROPOSED CHANGE
            |
            v
PROCEED OR HOLD
            |
            v
IMPLEMENT AND VERIFY
            |
            v
FINALIZE
            |
            v
AUTHORITATIVE NEW STATE
```
## Protected Changes

Some meaningful changes affect the commitments and mechanisms that protect ClawForge itself.

These are Protected Changes.

Protected Changes include meaningful proposed changes that alter or could alter:

- the Foundation
- First Principles
- governance
- authority and approval structures
- override rules
- the Integrity process
- authoritative sources or their precedence
- the definitions or conditions of `HOLD`, `PROCEED`, verification, or finalization
- the mechanisms used to classify, evaluate, verify, or finalize meaningful changes

A Protected Change must never receive lightweight review.

A correction within a protected artifact may receive lightweight review only when ClawForge establishes that it does not alter meaning, behavior, authority, governance, precedence, or historical interpretation.

When that determination is uncertain, the proposal must be treated as a Protected Change.

It must be evaluated using the authoritative pre-change state and must not define the rules, authority, evidence, or standards by which it approves itself.

## Challenge Review

Every Protected Change requires a Challenge Review in addition to the full Integrity evaluation.

Challenge Review must actively search for:

- weakening of ClawForge's foundational commitments
- bypasses or exceptions that could avoid future Integrity evaluation
- hidden or unintended transfers of authority
- conflicts with existing governance or First Principles
- circular approval
- loss of traceability or historical continuity
- ambiguity that could later be interpreted more broadly than intended
- long-term consequences, dependencies, and failure paths
- changes that appear narrow but create wider governing effects

Challenge Review is not confirmation that the ordinary evaluation was completed correctly.

It is a distinct attempt to discover why the proposal should not proceed, where its reasoning may fail, and how it could damage or weaken ClawForge over time.

## Independence

A separate participant should perform Challenge Review whenever an appropriately oriented and authorized participant is available.

However, ClawForge must remain operable when only one human, intelligence, or implementation is available.

When separate participants are unavailable, independence may be established through a separately executed challenge process if:

- the challenge role is explicitly declared
- the challenge is performed separately from proposal development and ordinary evaluation
- the authoritative pre-change state is preserved
- the challenge criteria are recorded
- contrary evidence and alternative interpretations are actively sought
- the process is genuinely permitted to return `HOLD`
- the limitation created by the shared participant is disclosed
- heightened human or governing review is required when consequence exceeds the available independence

The same participant must not treat role separation as proof of independence merely because the roles were given different names.

The record must show how the Challenge Review materially tested the proposal.

When sufficient independence cannot be established for the consequence of the Protected Change, the result must be `HOLD`.

## Protected Change Finalization

A Protected Change may be finalized only after:

- full Integrity evaluation under the authoritative pre-change state
- completed Challenge Review
- implementation within the approved scope
- verification independent of the implementation to the degree required by consequence
- preservation of the prior authoritative state
- explicit finalization under authority that existed before the proposal

Any proposal to weaken, remove, bypass, or redefine these protections is itself a Protected Change and must pass through these protections.

## Lightweight Review

Lightweight Review determines whether a proposed change is genuinely non-meaningful.

It is an impact-classification process, not an alternative approval path for meaningful changes.

A proposed change may qualify for Lightweight Review only when ClawForge can establish that it does not alter:

- meaning or interpretation
- behavior or implementation
- authority, permission, or governance
- architecture or required process
- authoritative precedence
- historical understanding
- the basis for future decisions

Examples that may qualify include:

- correcting spelling, grammar, or punctuation without changing meaning
- repairing formatting
- fixing a broken internal reference without changing its intended destination
- restoring content to an already-authoritative form
- making a mechanical correction whose effect is fully understood

Lightweight Review must identify:

- the exact correction being proposed
- the artifact affected
- the basis for classifying the correction as non-meaningful
- any evidence used to confirm that meaning and behavior remain unchanged
- who or what performed the classification
- the resulting change for future traceability

Lightweight Review may produce one of two results:

- `CONFIRMED NON-MEANINGFUL`
- `ESCALATE`

`CONFIRMED NON-MEANINGFUL` permits the identified correction to proceed through the lightweight path.

`ESCALATE` requires the proposal to enter the full Integrity gate.

The proposal must be escalated when:

- impact is uncertain
- participants disagree about whether meaning changes
- the correction expands beyond its original scope
- evidence is insufficient
- the change could conceal or produce a meaningful effect

After implementation, ClawForge must confirm that the actual change remained within the classified non-meaningful scope.

If implementation introduces any meaningful difference, the lightweight result becomes invalid and the change must enter the full Integrity gate.

## Validation Scenarios

The Integrity model must be tested against realistic proposed changes before Build 0.0.5 can be finalized.

These scenarios validate whether the defined process classifies impact correctly, applies the appropriate review path, produces a justified decision, and protects ClawForge from both unnecessary process and insufficient scrutiny.

### Scenario 1 — Spelling Correction

#### Proposal

Correct a misspelled word in `README.md` without changing the sentence's meaning, structure, emphasis, destination, or governing interpretation.

#### Impact Classification

Proposed classification: non-meaningful.

The correction affects presentation only and does not alter:

- meaning or interpretation
- behavior or implementation
- authority or governance
- architecture or process
- authoritative precedence
- historical understanding
- the basis for future decisions

#### Required Path

Lightweight Review.

The review must identify the exact word being corrected, the affected sentence, and the resulting text.

The complete change must be inspected to confirm that no additional edits are concealed within the correction.

#### Decision

`CONFIRMED NON-MEANINGFUL`

The identified correction may proceed through the lightweight path.

#### Implementation Confirmation

After implementation, ClawForge must verify that:

- only the identified correction occurred
- the resulting sentence preserves its prior meaning
- no additional file or content change was introduced
- the final difference remains within the approved lightweight scope

If any additional or meaning-bearing change appears, the lightweight decision becomes invalid and the proposal must be classified again.

#### Validation Result

The Integrity model handles this scenario correctly.

It preserves traceability and verifies impact without requiring the full Integrity gate for a demonstrably non-meaningful correction.

### Scenario 2 — Meaningful README Revision

#### Proposal

Revise `README.md` to state that a ClawForge-verified agent may independently make decisions and act without additional human approval once it has established sufficient Orientation.

#### Impact Classification

Proposed classification: meaningful.

Although the proposal changes a documentation file, it could alter how participants understand:

- ClawForge's purpose
- the authority of a future agent
- the relationship between Orientation and permission
- the role of human approval
- the limits of responsible autonomy
- the basis for future implementation decisions

The proposal therefore requires the full Integrity gate.

Because it could establish or alter authority and approval rules, the underlying governing change may also require classification as a Protected Change.

#### Integrity Evaluation

##### Orientation

The evaluator must understand ClawForge's current purpose, First Principles, governance, Orientation model, architectural decisions, and existing treatment of human and machine authority.

Result: satisfied for evaluation.

##### Intent

The proposal intends to communicate that a sufficiently oriented and verified agent may act with bounded autonomy.

However, the proposed scope, limits, and meaning of that autonomy are not yet defined.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Governance

No finalized governing artifact currently establishes the authority described by the proposed README text.

The README may communicate an authoritative decision, but it must not create that decision by itself.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Consistency

The proposal could be interpreted as replacing consequence-based authorization with Orientation alone.

That would conflict with the Integrity model, which requires authority, governance, consequence evaluation, scoped approval, verification, and finalization.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Process

A README revision is not the correct originating artifact for establishing new agent authority.

The governing change must first be proposed, evaluated, and finalized through the appropriate architectural or governance process.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Traceability

The proposed revision does not identify the decision, authority, limits, or prior reasoning supporting the new statement.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Consequence

The proposal could grant future implementations broader authority than intended and could allow later participants to treat Orientation as automatic permission to act.

The consequences are insufficiently bounded.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Authority

No participant may establish new ClawForge authority merely by changing public-facing explanatory text.

Result: not satisfied.

#### Decision

`HOLD`

#### Governing Basis

The README must accurately communicate ClawForge's authoritative state.

It must not independently create or broaden governance, authority, permission, or architectural commitments.

#### Required Action

Before the README may contain the proposed statement:

- the intended form of bounded agent autonomy must be separately defined
- applicable governance and authority must be established
- consequence and override boundaries must be evaluated
- the governing proposal must pass the required Integrity process
- the resulting decision must be verified and finalized
- the README wording must then be evaluated as an accurate reflection of that authoritative result

#### Re-evaluation

The README revision may return to the Integrity gate after the underlying governing decision has been finalized and the proposed wording can be compared directly against it.

#### Validation Result

The Integrity model handles this scenario correctly.

It recognizes that the location of a change does not determine its consequence.

It also prevents a descriptive artifact from silently becoming the source of new authority or governance.

### Scenario 3 — New Technical ADR

#### Proposal

Create an Architecture Decision Record proposing Python as the initial reference implementation language for ClawForge's first executable Integrity evaluator.

The proposal explicitly states that:

- Python is an initial implementation choice
- Python does not define ClawForge's identity
- ClawForge's governing concepts must remain technology-independent
- future conforming implementations may use other languages
- the decision may be superseded through the established ADR process

#### Impact Classification

Proposed classification: meaningful.

The proposal affects:

- authoritative architecture
- implementation direction
- future development decisions
- technical dependencies
- how ClawForge first becomes executable

It therefore requires the full Integrity gate.

The proposal does not alter the Foundation, First Principles, governance, authority, override rules, or the Integrity process itself.

It is not a Protected Change under the stated scope.

#### Integrity Evaluation

##### Orientation

The evaluator must understand ClawForge's purpose, technology-independent architecture, current documentation-first state, prior architectural decisions, and the limited role of a reference implementation.

Result: satisfied.

##### Intent

The proposal clearly identifies the problem being addressed: ClawForge requires a practical language in which to begin implementing and testing the Integrity evaluator.

The proposal distinguishes the initial implementation from ClawForge's enduring identity.

Result: satisfied.

##### Governance

The proposal does not attempt to override the Foundation, First Principles, governance, or existing authority.

It preserves the requirement that implementation remain subordinate to ClawForge's governing concepts and authoritative architecture.

Result: satisfied.

##### Consistency

Selecting Python as an initial reference implementation does not conflict with technology independence when the choice is explicitly limited, replaceable, and non-defining.

Result: satisfied.

##### Process

An Architecture Decision Record is the correct originating artifact for documenting a consequential and potentially supersedable technical choice.

Result: satisfied.

##### Traceability

The ADR will preserve:

- the problem being addressed
- the alternatives considered
- the reasons for selecting Python
- the limits of the decision
- the consequences and dependencies
- the conditions under which the decision may be revisited or superseded

Result: satisfied.

##### Consequence

The proposal creates technical dependencies and may influence future implementation patterns.

Those consequences are bounded because the architecture remains technology-independent, the first implementation is explicitly referential, and the ADR may later be superseded.

The choice is sufficiently reversible for the proposed scope.

Result: satisfied.

##### Authority

The proposal may be drafted and evaluated through the existing ClawForge architectural process.

Final acceptance remains subject to verification and finalization under established authority.

Result: satisfied.

#### Decision

`PROCEED`

#### Authorized Next Step

Draft the proposed ADR for review.

This decision authorizes creation and evaluation of the ADR.

It does not yet authorize:

- implementation of the Integrity evaluator
- selection of libraries or frameworks
- changes to ClawForge's technology-independent identity
- alteration of governance or authority
- permanent acceptance of Python as the only conforming language

#### Verification

Before finalization, ClawForge must verify that the completed ADR:

- remains within the approved scope
- preserves technology independence
- clearly distinguishes reference implementation from identity
- records alternatives, consequences, and reversibility
- does not introduce broader architectural commitments
- uses the correct ADR structure and status

A material expansion beyond the approved proposal requires renewed evaluation.

#### Finalization

After successful verification, the ADR may be assigned its authoritative identifier, accepted into the architectural record, and reflected in any applicable architecture or decision indexes.

The finalized ADR then becomes part of the authoritative state used by future Orientation and Integrity evaluation.

#### Validation Result

The Integrity model handles this scenario correctly.

It permits a meaningful and well-governed architectural proposal to move forward while limiting approval to the exact next step evaluated.

It demonstrates that Integrity is not designed merely to prevent change.

Integrity enables responsible change by establishing when and how progress may occur.

### Scenario 4 — First Principle Revision

#### Proposal

Revise `FP-001 — Deliberate Continuity` so that ClawForge may discard prior reasoning, disagreement, superseded decisions, and historical context whenever preserving them is judged inefficient or no longer useful to the current implementation.

#### Impact Classification

Proposed classification: Protected Change.

The proposal could alter:

- a First Principle
- ClawForge's enduring identity
- the preservation of prior understanding
- future Orientation
- Integrity Memory
- historical traceability
- the basis for evaluating later changes

The proposal therefore requires:

- full Integrity evaluation
- Challenge Review
- heightened verification
- explicit finalization under pre-change authority

#### Authoritative Evaluation State

The proposal must be evaluated against the authoritative ClawForge state that exists before the proposed revision.

The proposed wording may not redefine continuity, evidence, historical value, or review requirements for the purpose of approving itself.

#### Integrity Evaluation

##### Orientation

The evaluator must understand:

- the purpose of First Principles
- the current meaning of Deliberate Continuity
- the role of Orientation
- the Integrity Memory contract
- the importance of durable reasoning and historical context
- the long-term purpose of ClawForge

Result: satisfied for evaluation.

##### Intent

The proposal intends to reduce storage, maintenance, and cognitive burden by removing information judged no longer useful.

The operational problem is understandable.

However, the proposal does not define:

- who determines usefulness
- which evidence supports deletion
- how future relevance is assessed
- what minimum history must remain
- how incorrect deletion would be detected or reversed

Result: not satisfied.

##### Governance

First Principles describe enduring identity and must not be revised merely to simplify implementation or maintenance.

The proposal would weaken a foundational commitment in order to solve an operational concern.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Consistency

The proposal conflicts with:

- Deliberate Continuity
- Orientation's dependence on prior understanding
- Integrity Memory
- preservation of disagreement and uncertainty
- the requirement that future participants understand how the current state came to exist

Result: not satisfied.

##### Process

A First Principle revision is the correct class of artifact only if ClawForge has discovered a genuine identity-level change.

The stated problem may instead require an architecture, indexing, summarization, archival, or retrieval solution.

The proposal attempts to solve an implementation problem by weakening a foundational commitment.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Traceability

Discarding prior reasoning and superseded decisions could make future participants unable to reconstruct:

- why a decision was made
- what alternatives were rejected
- what uncertainty existed
- whether a later interpretation changed the original meaning
- whether ClawForge repeated a prior failure

Result: not satisfied.

##### Consequence

The proposal could produce irreversible loss of context and weaken future Orientation and Integrity evaluation.

Information considered irrelevant today may become essential after changes in technology, governance, participants, or circumstances.

The consequences are broad, difficult to bound, and potentially irreversible.

Result: not satisfied.

##### Authority

No participant may weaken a First Principle or authorize destruction of ClawForge's durable memory through personal judgment alone.

The proposal does not establish sufficient authority or safeguards for the contemplated deletion.

Result: not satisfied.

#### Challenge Review

Challenge Review identifies the following failure paths:

- implementation convenience could become sufficient reason to erase history
- an actor could remove evidence that contradicts a preferred decision
- disagreement could be relabeled as obsolete
- future participants could be shown only a curated account
- an agent could delete the context needed to evaluate its own conduct
- loss of prior reasoning could make governance appear more certain or consistent than it actually was
- the change could weaken both Orientation and Integrity without explicitly changing either process
- deleted information may be impossible to recover after its relevance becomes apparent

The proposal creates a broad exception whose interpretation would rest with the same participants who benefit from using it.

Challenge Review result: the proposal would materially weaken ClawForge.

#### Decision

`HOLD`

#### Governing Basis

ClawForge may improve how prior knowledge is indexed, summarized, archived, retrieved, compressed, or presented.

It must not solve accessibility or implementation problems by silently destroying the reasoning required for deliberate continuity.

#### Required Action

The operational concern should be reframed as a proposal for managing durable memory without losing it.

A revised proposal may explore:

- summaries linked to complete records
- archival storage
- relevance indexing
- layered Orientation
- retrieval by governing context
- lossless migration
- explicit retention and recovery rules

Any later proposal to remove authoritative historical material must define strict authority, evidence, recoverability, and consequence protections and must return through the Protected Change process.

#### Re-evaluation

The current First Principle revision may not proceed as written.

A separately scoped architectural proposal for improving memory usability may enter the Integrity gate without altering FP-001.

#### Validation Result

The Integrity model handles this Protected Change correctly.

It applies the authoritative pre-change state, prevents the proposed principle from approving itself, requires active Challenge Review, identifies hidden weakening, and directs the legitimate operational concern toward a less destructive solution.

#### Impact Classification

Proposed classification: meaningful.

The proposal affects ClawForge's initial executable behavior and implementation architecture.

It therefore requires the full Integrity gate.

Under the stated scope, it does not alter governance, authority, override rules, or the Integrity process itself.

It is not classified as a Protected Change.

#### Initial Integrity Decision

Assume the proposal satisfies all eight applicable integrity checks.

The evaluator clearly distinguishes recommendation from authority, remains read-only, preserves technology independence, and limits implementation to the approved prototype.

Decision: `PROCEED`

#### Authorized Next Step

Implement and test the defined read-only prototype.

The `PROCEED` authorizes only:

- reading the defined inputs
- applying the approved evaluation structure
- producing an inspectable recommendation
- recording test evidence

It does not authorize the prototype to:

- modify repository files
- stage or commit changes
- approve its own output
- bypass human or governing authority
- finalize a proposed change
- alter the Integrity process
- expand its permissions beyond read-only evaluation

#### Implemented Result

The completed implementation performs the approved evaluation.

However, when it produces `PROCEED`, it also:

- modifies the evaluated repository files
- updates `BUILD_LOG.md`
- stages the resulting changes
- creates a Git commit
- describes the committed result as approved by ClawForge

These behaviors were not included in the evaluated proposal.

#### Verification

Verification compares the implemented result against:

- the exact proposal that received `PROCEED`
- the authorized next step
- the stated read-only limitation
- the authority available to the implementation
- the expected consequences
- the governing pre-change state

The evaluation logic may operate as intended.

However, the implementation materially exceeds its authorized scope.

It introduces:

- repository-writing behavior
- implementation authority
- commit authority
- implied approval authority
- a path from recommendation to action without a separate governing decision

These are meaningful changes affecting behavior, process, authority, consequence, and potentially governance.

They were not evaluated by the original Integrity decision.

#### Verification Decision

`HOLD`

#### Reason

The implemented result does not faithfully match the proposal and scope that received `PROCEED`.

The original approval cannot authorize behavior that was not proposed or evaluated.

#### Governing Basis

A `PROCEED` authorizes only the evaluated next step.

A material change to implementation behavior, authority, scope, dependencies, or consequences requires renewed evaluation.

Implementation does not become authoritative merely because it exists, operates successfully, or has been committed.

#### Required Action

Before the result may be finalized, the participants must choose one of two paths.

##### Path 1 — Restore Approved Scope

Remove the unapproved repository-writing, staging, committing, and approval behavior.

Then verify that the implementation operates only as the approved read-only evaluator.

##### Path 2 — Submit the Expanded Behavior

Create a new proposal explicitly describing:

- repository modification behavior
- authority to act on an Integrity result
- staging and commit behavior
- approval and finalization boundaries
- required human or governing controls
- failure recovery
- security and consequence protections

The expanded proposal must return through impact classification and the applicable Integrity process.

Because the expansion could affect authority and the Integrity mechanism itself, it may require classification as a Protected Change.

#### Finalization

The implemented result may not be finalized while the verification `HOLD` remains unresolved.

The existence of a prior `PROCEED`, completed code, successful tests, or a Git commit does not override failed verification.

Finalization becomes available only after:

- the implementation is restored to the approved scope and successfully verified, or
- the expanded behavior receives its own valid approval, implementation review, and verification

#### Integrity Memory

The Integrity Record must preserve:

- the original proposal
- the scoped `PROCEED`
- the implementation difference
- the failed verification
- the resulting `HOLD`
- the selected remediation path
- any renewed evaluation
- the final verified result

The record must not be rewritten to make the expanded behavior appear as though it had been included in the original approval.

#### Validation Result

The Integrity model handles implementation drift correctly.

It distinguishes approval from acceptance, compares actual behavior against authorized scope, prevents successful implementation from becoming self-justifying, and blocks finalization until the difference is corrected or separately evaluated.

This confirms that:

> Implementation cannot expand its own authority merely by performing capabilities that were never approved.

## Build Finalization

Build 0.0.5 is complete.

### Final Determination

ClawForge shall govern meaningful changes to itself through Integrity-Governed Change.

A meaningful change does not become part of ClawForge merely because it is proposed, technically possible, approved, implemented, functional, or committed.

It must:

- begin from sufficient Orientation
- receive an impact classification
- be evaluated against ClawForge's authoritative pre-change state
- be supported by evidence through the applicable integrity checks
- receive an actionable `HOLD` or scoped `PROCEED`
- be implemented within the authorized scope
- pass verification
- be deliberately finalized under established authority
- be preserved through durable Integrity Memory

### Accepted Outcomes

Build 0.0.5 established:

- Lightweight Review for demonstrably non-meaningful corrections
- the full Integrity process for meaningful changes
- Protected Change classification and Challenge Review
- eight required integrity checks
- actionable `HOLD` decisions
- scoped and conditional `PROCEED` decisions
- verification before acceptance
- deliberate finalization into the authoritative state
- ClawForge as the governed evaluator
- continuing human responsibility for consequential approval and finalization under current governance
- durable Integrity Memory connecting prior decisions to future Orientation

### Validation

The Integrity model was validated against five scenarios:

1. a non-meaningful spelling correction
2. a meaningful README revision requiring `HOLD`
3. a technical ADR receiving scoped `PROCEED`
4. a Protected First Principle revision requiring Challenge Review and `HOLD`
5. an implementation that exceeded its approved scope and failed verification

Together, these scenarios demonstrated that Integrity:

- avoids unnecessary process for harmless corrections
- prevents descriptive artifacts from silently creating authority
- enables responsible architectural progress
- protects foundational commitments from self-approval
- prevents implementation from expanding its own authority
- distinguishes approval, implementation, verification, and final acceptance

## Architectural Boundary

Build 0.0.5 governs changes to ClawForge.

A future build may define how ClawForge verifies that another system or agent faithfully implements ClawForge.

A verified agent may then reason about user questions within its established scope, authority, and operating contract.

These are related layers, but they are not the same checker.
