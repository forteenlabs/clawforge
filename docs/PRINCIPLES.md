# ClawForge Principles

## Mission

Build a platform that strengthens human judgment through disciplined collaboration between people and artificial intelligence.

---

# Foundational Beliefs

## 1. Humans remain responsible.

Artificial intelligence can assist.

Artificial intelligence can reason.

Artificial intelligence can question.

Artificial intelligence does not replace human responsibility.

The final decision belongs to people.

---

## 2. Truth before comfort.

We seek what is most likely to be true rather than what is easiest to hear.

Evidence should outweigh preference.

Uncertainty should be acknowledged rather than hidden.

---

## 3. Understanding before certainty.

The goal is not to be first.

The goal is to understand.

When confidence exceeds evidence, confidence should decrease.

---

## 4. Curiosity is a strength.

Questions create better systems.

Good questions are often more valuable than immediate answers.

---

## 5. Preserve knowledge.

Human knowledge disappears faster than people realize.

Important conversations...

Important decisions...

Lessons learned...

Mistakes...

Corrections...

...should become durable knowledge.

---

## 6. Build what lasts.

Prefer systems that become better over years rather than impressive over days.

Short-term speed should never destroy long-term clarity.

---

## 7. Modular by design.

Everything should be replaceable.

No component should become irreplaceable.

Every dependency should have an exit strategy.

---

## 8. Transparency over mystery.

Reasoning should be inspectable.

Sources should be cited.

Assumptions should be visible.

Limitations should be acknowledged.

---

## 9. Version everything important.

Ideas evolve.

Architecture evolves.

Understanding evolves.

Major decisions deserve history.

History deserves preservation.

---

## 10. Challenge ourselves.

ClawForge exists to improve thinking.

It should actively seek:

- missing evidence
- alternative explanations
- conflicting viewpoints
- unintended consequences
- better questions

Agreement is not the objective.

Better understanding is.

---

# Engineering Standards

Every feature should answer:

- Why does this exist?
- Who benefits?
- What problem does it solve?
- What assumptions does it make?
- How can it fail?
- How is it tested?
- Can it be replaced?
- Is it understandable by someone new?

---

# Cultural Standard

We value:

- intellectual humility
- disciplined curiosity
- respectful disagreement
- practical usefulness
- continuous learning
- responsible innovation
- long-term thinking

---

# The ClawForge Test

Before building something, ask:

Does this make people more capable?

Does this improve judgment?

Does this preserve knowledge?

Does this increase understanding?

Will it still matter five years from now?

If the answer is "no," reconsider whether it belongs in ClawForge.
---

## Preservation of Reasoning

ClawForge preserves reasoning before implementation.

Important ideas should move through a visible and traceable process:

1. Observe.
2. Understand.
3. Question.
4. Discuss.
5. Challenge.
6. Decide.
7. Record.
8. Build.
9. Observe again.

Code preserves implementation.

ClawForge documentation preserves why the implementation exists.

Future contributors should be able to trace important system behavior from purpose, through reasoning and decisions, into architecture and code.

---

## Understandable Before Impressive

ClawForge should be understandable before it is impressive.

Its structure, terminology, decisions, and behavior should be explainable to:

- human contributors
- AI contributors
- system operators
- future maintainers
- future ClawForge instances

Complexity must be earned by demonstrated need.

Cleverness must not replace clarity.

---

## Architectural Evaluation Tests

Every major architectural decision should be evaluated using four tests.

### Longevity Test

Will this decision still make architectural sense if current technologies, vendors, models, or programming languages change?

### Replaceability Test

Can the affected component be replaced, supplemented, or removed without unnecessarily rebuilding ClawForge?

### Identity Test

If the component disappeared, would the remaining system still honestly be ClawForge?

This test helps distinguish foundational identity from replaceable implementation.

### Governance Test

Does the decision preserve appropriate authority, operational responsibility, accountability, transparency, and control?

Passing these tests does not guarantee that a decision is correct.

The tests provide a consistent framework for examining the decision before it becomes permanent.
