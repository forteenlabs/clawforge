## Major Developments

### Blueprint Established

`docs/BLUEPRINT.md` was created as the active workspace for architectural questions, discussion, alternatives, and provisional decisions.

The first Blueprint question asks:

> What is the smallest executable that still deserves to be called ClawForge?

A provisional decision was reached that the first executable should be a provider-independent, governed request-processing loop.

The initial executable should include:

- a participant request
- participant and request context
- a policy checkpoint
- core orchestration
- a provider-neutral interface
- a deterministic initial provider
- a structured response
- an execution record

The first executable is intended to prove ClawForge’s architecture rather than demonstrate advanced artificial intelligence.

---

## Participants and Intelligence

ClawForge should accept requests from participants rather than assuming that every requester is human.

Possible participants may include:

- humans
- AI systems
- software agents
- automated services
- other ClawForge instances
- future forms of intelligence

Authority, operational responsibility, and accountability were identified as separate governance concepts.

Under current governance, humans retain ultimate moral, ethical, and legal accountability.

AI may receive delegated authority and operational responsibility within established policies.

The complete future responsibility model remains intentionally unresolved.

---

## ClawForge Identity

ClawForge should not be defined by:

- a specific AI provider
- a specific model
- a specific programming language
- a specific user interface

ClawForge should remain recognizable when those components are replaced.

A central working distinction was established:

> Governance provides continuity. Intelligence provides adaptability.

ClawForge may eventually express a persistent artificial-intelligence identity through its combination of governance, memory, reasoning, tools, history, and operating principles.

However, no individual provider or model is ClawForge itself.

---

## Architectural Evaluation Framework

Four tests were established for evaluating major architectural decisions.

### Longevity Test

Will the decision remain architecturally meaningful as technologies, providers, languages, and contributors change?

### Replaceability Test

Can the affected component be replaced, supplemented, or removed without unnecessarily rebuilding ClawForge?

### Identity Test

Would the remaining system still honestly be ClawForge if the component disappeared?

### Governance Test

Does the decision preserve appropriate authority, operational responsibility, accountability, transparency, and control?

# Foundation Layer Established

During architectural exploration, ClawForge reached a significant realization.

Architectural Decision Records explain how architectural decisions are preserved.

They do not explain why those kinds of decisions should exist in the first place.

Governance itself required a deeper foundation.

This resulted in the creation of a new architectural layer:

```
Vision
    │
    ▼
Foundation
    │
    ▼
Governance
    │
    ▼
Architecture
    │
    ▼
Engineering
    │
    ▼
Implementation
```

This was the first major structural change to ClawForge's architecture since Build 0.0.3 began.

---

## Discovery

The original discussion centered around two ideas.

Should ClawForge exist primarily to:

- discover better reasoning,

or

- preserve reasoning for future contributors?

Through discussion, these were recognized as complementary rather than competing goals.

Discovery without continuity repeatedly loses understanding.

Continuity without continued discovery eventually stagnates.

This realization became FP-001:

> Deliberate Continuity.

## Pressure Testing

The emerging Foundation was tested across numerous domains including:

- science,
- mathematics,
- economics,
- software engineering,
- philosophy,
- organizational design,
- religion as a process example,
- and hypothetical future civilizations.

The purpose was never to validate any particular worldview.

The purpose was to determine whether the architectural principle itself remained valid independent of the example.

It consistently did.

---

## Self-Correction

The most important discovery of the session was that ClawForge must be capable of correcting itself without losing its identity.

This led to an explicit requirement:

No First Principle is exempt from deliberate re-evaluation.

Any future revision must preserve:

- historical lineage,
- previous reasoning,
- supporting evidence,
- and the explanation for why change became necessary.

The Foundation therefore protects understanding rather than conclusions.

## Git

This session also established the team's standard Git workflow.

```
Working Directory
        ↓
git add
        ↓
Staging Area
        ↓
git commit
        ↓
Repository History
        ↓
git push
        ↓

```

An important lesson:

`git add` stages a snapshot.

Editing a file afterward requires staging it again before committing.

This mirrors ClawForge's own philosophy of deliberate, inspectable progression.
